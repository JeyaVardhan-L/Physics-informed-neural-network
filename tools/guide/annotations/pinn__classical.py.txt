== """What a physicist would do
A comparison with only a weak opponent proves little. This is the strong one: the same dots and the same physics, solved exactly.

== def basis
One row per time, two columns: the decaying cosine and the decaying sine. Every solution of the equation is a mix of these two columns.

== def fit
Two unknowns, ten equations, linear. `lstsq` uses the SVD and takes microseconds.

== def expected_future_mse
How much error should be expected from this fit, averaged over the noise? Noise of variance s² in the dots gives the coefficients a covariance of s²(BᵀB)⁻¹. Multiplying by the basis at future times turns that into a variance of the prediction.

The Gauss-Markov theorem says no linear unbiased estimator has a smaller variance. For Gaussian noise no unbiased estimator of any kind does.
