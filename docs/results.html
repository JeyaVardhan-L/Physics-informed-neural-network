Chapter 07

What it scored, and against what

A ratio is only as good as its denominator. Here are three of them.

Ten seeds. Each has its own noise, so its own ten dots, and its own starting weights. Five methods answer the same question on each. The settings were fixed on other seeds beforehand.

Three denominators

Against the plain net the PINN's median error is about 440,000 times lower. Taken seed by seed the factor runs from 14,000 to 5,100,000. A number that moves that much is mostly a measurement of how wildly the plain net missed. Train the plain net with L-BFGS as well and the ratio of medians becomes 1,300,000, with the PINN untouched.

Against always zero it is about 21,000 times lower. This denominator is a property of the spring and can't be made worse by training it badly, so it is the fairer headline.

Against least squares the PINN's median is 1.4 times higher. This is the number that says how good the PINN is. Least squares uses the same dots and the same equation and is the best that can be done with them.

The noise floor

With m, c and k known, the curve is a u(t) + b w(t), where u and w are the decaying cosine and sine. Put them in two columns of a matrix B, one row for each dot. The best estimate of (a, b) is least squares, and noise of variance σ² gives it the covariance

Cov(a, b) = σ² (BTB)−1

Push that through the same columns evaluated at future times and average. The result is 5.5 × 10−6. A test simulates 2,000 noisy data sets and checks that the formula agrees. The Gauss-Markov theorem says no linear unbiased method does better on average.

The floor is an average over noise, so single seeds land on both sides of it. On some seeds the PINN even beats least squares. That is luck in the noise: no method can beat it on average.

The lowest points in the figure also say something about the claim. The PINN can't be a thousand times better than this, however it is trained, because the dots don't contain the information. With noise this size, the remaining error belongs to the measurements.

IN:How much better is the PINN?
OUT:

About 21,000 times better than predicting nothing, and within a factor of 1.4 of the best method possible. The ratio to the plain net is larger and means less, because the plain net's error is nearly arbitrary. If someone quotes only that ratio, ask how the baseline was trained.
