Chapter 09

Running it backwards

The dots are known and the spring is not. The same loss finds both the curve and the constants.

So far the law was known completely. In practice the form of a law is often known while some number in it is not: a friction coefficient, a diffusion rate. Finding it from measurements is called an inverse problem.

With a PINN it takes a few extra lines. Make c and k trainable and give them to the optimiser next to the weights. They appear inside the residual, so the gradient of the loss reaches them through the same backward pass. the two parameters

Three decisions

Train the logarithms. c and k must be positive, and e to any power is. Adam moves each parameter by roughly the learning rate per step, so on a logarithm a step is a relative change, about the same for c near 3 and k near 225.

Scale by a constant. The residual is divided by the first guess for k, which is fixed. Dividing by the trainable k would change the exchange rate between the two losses during the search.

Leave m alone. Multiply m, c and k by the same number and the equation is unchanged. Two springs that differ in this way move identically, so no amount of position data can tell them apart. Only the ratios c/m and k/m are identifiable. Finding m itself would take a measurement of force.

In relative terms k is far better determined than c. Stiffness sets the timing of the swing, and ten dots see timing well: the moment the mass passes the middle, the moment it turns. Friction shows only as a small loss of height over part of one swing, and noise can imitate that. Without noise both numbers come back to four digits, so the limit here is the data.

With 1% noise the error after the dots is 2.7 × 10−4, compared with 3.8 × 10−6 when c and k are given. Knowing the constants is worth that much.

IN:How does a PINN estimate an unknown coefficient?
OUT:

The coefficient becomes a trainable parameter inside the residual. The dots hold the curve in place, and if the coefficient is wrong, that curve can't satisfy the equation. The physics loss then has a gradient that pushes the coefficient towards a value that fits. I train its logarithm, so it stays positive. What can be recovered is limited by identifiability and by noise, and I would report the spread over seeds along with the estimate.
